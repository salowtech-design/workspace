Sheets

Kept in this browser only. Formulas start with =. Subset of spreadsheet features, no sharing.

Supported functions and limits

Functions: SUM, AVERAGE, MIN, MAX, COUNT, ABS, ROUND, LEN, UPPER, LOWER, CONCAT, IF, AND, OR, NOT, LEFT, RIGHT, MID, TRIM, SUBSTITUTE, FIND, DATE, YEAR, MONTH, DAY, WEEKDAY, MOD, POWER, SQRT, INT, FLOOR, CEILING, MEDIAN, STDEV, COUNTA, COUNTBLANK, VLOOKUP, INDEX, MATCH, SUMIF, COUNTIF, AVERAGEIF, SUMIFS, COUNTIFS, AVERAGEIFS, ROUNDUP, ROUNDDOWN, TRUNC, SIGN, PRODUCT, LARGE, SMALL, ISNUMBER, ISTEXT, ISBLANK, ISERROR, IFERROR. Operators + - * / ^ & % and comparisons. This is a subset, not full Excel or Google Sheets compatibility. The visible grid is A1 to Z100.

Lookups: row and column numbers start at 1. VLOOKUP and MATCH search approximately by default, which needs sorted data and scans top to bottom (unsorted data gives different answers than Excel or Google Sheets), so use FALSE or 0 for an exact match. Nothing found is #N/A. INDEX takes a row and a column; one index is the row and works on a one-column range, and 0 (a whole row or column) is not supported. Text matching ignores case and accepts ? and * (~ escapes them); the same works in COUNTIF, SUMIF, AVERAGEIF and the multi-criteria SUMIFS, COUNTIFS and AVERAGEIFS (every pair must match, all ranges the same size, up to 10 pairs). Deviations from Excel and Google Sheets, on purpose and not parity: ISBLANK is also true for an empty-text cell and ISTEXT is false for empty text; LARGE and SMALL give #VALUE! for k of 0 or less and #NUM! for k above the count; ROUNDUP, ROUNDDOWN, TRUNC and ROUND clamp the digit count to between -15 and 15 (so ROUNDUP(x,-24) rounds as -15 would), and a number too large to shift is returned unchanged; PRODUCT over about 1.8e308 is #VALUE!. IFERROR(value, fallback) runs the fallback only on an error and gives empty text if you omit it. The criteria functions skip cells that hold an error, including for "<>". An error in the criterion is returned as that error. SUMIF and AVERAGEIF need a sum range the same size as the range. Wildcard matching counts against the work limit and gives #LIMIT! when too heavy, and lookup text over 255 characters is #VALUE!.

Text: LEFT, RIGHT and MID count UTF-16 units like LEN, so an emoji counts as 2 and cutting it leaves half of it. A half emoji cannot be saved in a CSV file, so the download writes the replacement character (U+FFFD) in its place and says how many. FIND and SUBSTITUTE are case-sensitive with no wildcards. FIND not found is #VALUE!. TRIM collapses runs of spaces only. A text function given a range of more than one cell is #VALUE!.

Dates are whole-day serial numbers: day 0 is 1899-12-30, as in Google Sheets, so DATE(1970,1,1) is 25569. DATE rolls a month or day out of range over (month 13 is next January), cuts decimals, and adds 1900 to a year from 0 to 1899. YEAR, MONTH, DAY and WEEKDAY take a serial number (typed date text is just text and gives #VALUE!). A time of day after the point is dropped. Dates run from year 0 to 9999, otherwise #NUM!. WEEKDAY types 1, 2, 3 and 11 to 17 are supported. There is no TODAY, NOW, DATEVALUE, EDATE, TEXT or date format, and the results do not depend on your time zone.

Dates and Excel: the epoch is Google's, so DATE(1900,1,1) is 2. Excel counts 1900-01-01 as day 1 and treats 1900-02-29 as a real day (Microsoft documents this, and says WEEKDAY is wrong before 1900-03-01 for that reason), so a serial before 1900-03-01 differs by one day from Excel. This is not modelled. Dates typed as text are not converted, and there is no date display: a date shows as its serial number.

Statistics: MEDIAN (the mean of the two middle numbers when the count is even, #NUM! when there are no numbers) and STDEV (sample deviation, n minus 1; #DIV/0! for fewer than two numbers) use numbers only and ignore text and blanks in a range. COUNTA counts every non-empty cell, text included, an error cell as one value, and cells holding an empty-text result. COUNTBLANK counts empty cells and empty-text results, and needs a range or cell. Results are ordinary double-precision numbers, so a value such as STDEV(0.1,0.2,0.3) can show 0.09999999999999999; ROUND it for display. The sheet is computed in this browser only and nothing here is checked against Google Sheets or Excel. Not available: STDEVP, VAR, MODE, QUARTILE, PERCENTILE, COUNTUNIQUE.

Math: MOD takes the sign of the divisor. With whole numbers MOD, FLOOR and CEILING use plain integer arithmetic with no rounding step, which was tested on the cases listed in the test table (for example 16-digit values); all numbers are ordinary double-precision, so there is no exactness guarantee beyond 2^53 (about 9e15). With decimals MOD can leave float noise (MOD(5.1,1) is 0.0999999999999996), so ROUND it; a remainder that is only the noise of an exact multiple is 0, so MOD(0.3,0.1) is 0. POWER of 0 to a negative power is #DIV/0! and a negative base with a fractional exponent is #NUM!, SQRT of a negative is #NUM!. The ^ operator gives #VALUE! in those cases and for overflow, where POWER gives #DIV/0! or #NUM!. FLOOR and CEILING default to a factor of 1, a zero factor is #DIV/0!, and a negative factor is #NUM! here (not supported).

Errors: #DIV/0!, #VALUE!, #NAME?, #REF!, #CIRC!, #PARSE!, #N/A, #NUM!, #LIMIT! (too deep or too much work).

Where this sheet is stored

Only in this browser, as a separate copy for each signed-in person, which keeps people apart inside the app. It is not sent to the server, not shared, not synced and not a backup. It is not encrypted against someone who can open this browser's storage. It stays on this device after you sign out until you clear it, and the browser can clear it. Use Export (.json) to keep a copy.

Tabs are stored in this browser only. Only the most recently deleted tab can be restored, and only until you reload. Keep one browser window on this sheet.

More about tabs

Up to 10 tabs. Each tab has its own cells. A formula can read another tab by name: =Sheet2!B3 or =SUM('Q1 sales'!A1:A9) (a name with spaces goes in single quotes; a quote inside a name is doubled). Names ignore capital letters. References are by name, so renaming or deleting a tab makes them show #REF! until a tab has that name again (Restore deleted tab brings them back), and the rename or delete asks first. A tab that cannot be read, or whose copy here differs from what is stored, shows #TAB!. Loops across tabs show #CIRC!. Rows and columns cannot be inserted or deleted on a tab that other formulas refer to. Another tab's values come from this page's copy, so an edit made in another window shows after a reload. Undo, Export (.json), the CSV download, the chart, Sort, Filter and Import work on the tab you are on. Import replaces only that tab. Export (.json) and the CSV download save one tab at a time, so to back up a whole file export each tab. A change to the tab list made in another window stops this one until you reload, and an edit to a tab that another window deleted is not saved.

Arrow keys move, Enter moves down, Esc cancels an edit, Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y undo and redo.

Paste a block copied from another spreadsheet into the focused cell: it fills down and right, one undo step. Blank pasted cells clear what was there. It is refused while a filter is on. A value starting with = becomes a formula, as if typed.

More about pasting

Cells past column Z or row 100 are left out. Cells a validation rule refuses are skipped, and if a tab's validation rules cannot be read the paste is refused. A pasted formula keeps its references exactly as written, they are not shifted. Values like $5 or 15% stay text, they are not turned into numbers. A line break inside a pasted cell becomes a space, because a cell holds one line. A quoted cell that does not close, or has text after its closing quote, refuses the whole paste. Up to 2600 cells and 200000 characters at a time. Pasted formatting is not carried over. The status line says how many cells were filled, cleared, left out or refused, and how many start with =. Pasting a single value, or pasting into the formula bar, works as usual.

Frozen rows and columns stay in view while you scroll the grid, under the column letters and beside the row numbers. It is a view setting for this tab only, kept in this browser. It is not in Export (.json) or the CSV download and Import does not change it. It counts rows and columns by position, so inserting or deleting a row or column does not move it. Deleting a tab removes its setting, and Restore deleted tab brings it back with the tab until you reload. Rows cannot be frozen while a filter is on, and a filter is refused while rows are frozen. Frozen columns work with a filter. Wide frozen columns can fill a small screen, so use few.

    A saved version is a named copy of this tab as it is now: its cells and its validation, colour and format rules. Restore replaces this tab's cells and those rules with the saved ones and Undo brings the current ones back until you reload. Up to 5 versions per tab, named 1 to 40 characters. They are kept only in this browser, next to the tab, so clearing the browser's site data removes them with the tab. This is not a backup and is not synced. The freeze setting, tab name, chart and filter are not in a version, and Export (.json) and CSV do not include versions. Deleting a tab removes its versions and Restore deleted tab brings them back with it, until you reload. Restore is refused while a filter is on. If the browser's storage is full, saving a version is refused and nothing changes, and a restore that cannot be fully stored is rolled back and says so; if that rollback itself cannot be confirmed, editing stays off until you use Restore in the notice that appears.

    Import CSV reads a comma separated file into this tab starting at A1 and replaces the cells of this tab (it asks first and Undo works). Everything is kept as text exactly as written, so numbers are not converted and dates are not recognised. A cell that starts with = is kept as text with a leading apostrophe and is not run as a formula, the same mark the CSV download adds. The apostrophe is real text in the cell: it shows in the cell and the formula bar, it stays when you edit, and it is not hidden the way some programs hide it. Delete it yourself if you want the text. A line break inside a cell becomes a space. Cells past column Z or row 100, and cells a validation rule refuses, are left out and counted. The file is read as UTF-8 only: bytes that are not valid UTF-8 show as the replacement character and the question before the import tells you how many. Before anything changes, a question lists how many cells will be left out or changed and says the tab's old cells are all replaced, including those in places the file leaves empty. Up to 1,000,000 characters, 5,000 rows and 200 columns. A file with a quote that does not close is refused. It has not been tried with files from Google Sheets, Excel or other programs, only with files this page wrote and hand-made ones. Formulas, formatting and several tabs are not in a CSV file.

    Sort and filter

    Sort moves whole rows of typed values and is saved like an edit (Undo works). It refuses a range that holds formulas. Formulas outside the range that refer to cells inside it keep their addresses, so their results can change after a sort. Going up, numbers come before text and blank cells go last; going down, text comes before numbers and blanks still go last. Text is compared as lowercase characters by code, not in any language's alphabet order (for example accented letters come after z). Sort moves hidden rows too. Filter only hides rows on screen and changes no data. It resets when you reload the page. Editing a cell can make its row disappear when the filter re-applies. Hidden rows are still included in Export, the CSV download, the chart and formulas (a SUM counts them). It uses the values shown, so formula results count.

    Insert or delete the row or column of the selected cell. Cells and formula references, and the ranges of validation, colour and format rules, move with it. A reference to a deleted row or column shows #REF!, and a range shrinks or grows. It is refused when something in the last row (100) or column (Z) would be pushed off, when a filter is on, or when rules cannot be read. Delete asks first and says how much it removes. It is one undo step, and Undo refuses if the cells or rules were changed in another window or after the step. The chart and pivot are cleared. The change writes four stored items (cells and the three rule lists) one after another with a recovery copy first. That is not all-or-nothing: if a write fails the page tries to put every item back and tells you what it could and could not do, and if something is left unfinished a Restore notice appears and editing stays off until you use it.

    Copies the first row or column, one undo step. In a copied formula, references move with the fill unless pinned with $ ($A$1, $A1, A$1). A reference that would move off the grid becomes #REF!. Pasting does not move references. Refused while a filter is on.

    Searches and changes only A1:Z100, the cells on screen. Cells stored outside it are counted but never listed or changed. Plain text, not patterns. Typed cells are searched by their text. Formula cells are searched by their shown result, and a match there is only counted, never changed. With the formulas box ticked, formula text is searched and changed instead, and the shown results are not searched. Number formats are not searched. A replacement is skipped when it would turn text into a formula, strip the = from a formula, leave a formula that cannot be read (#PARSE!), be too long, or break a validation rule. A formula replacement that gives an unreadable formula (#PARSE!) is skipped, but one that reads and shows another error, such as #REF! or #NAME?, is written and shows that error; undo restores it. Replace all is one undo step and is refused while a filter is on.

    Pivot

    A pivot groups the rows of a range by one column and totals another column. It reads the values shown now (a formula uses its result), compares group names exactly after trimming spaces (capitals matter), and keeps groups in the order they first appear, up to 20 groups. An empty group cell is its own group, shown in italics as (blank); the words (blank) typed in a cell are a different group. Count counts rows. Sum, average, smallest and largest use only numbers: other values are left out and counted. Hidden rows are included. The table is built when you press the button. It is cleared as soon as any cell or the tab changes, so it never shows old numbers. A sum or average too large to hold shows "too large". It is not saved, not written into any cell and not in the Export, CSV or chart.

    Colour rules

    A colour rule shades cells in a range by the value shown, for example numbers above 100 in red. It only changes how cells look: it does not change, block or move any value, and the first matching rule wins. Numbers are compared as shown (a formula uses its result). Text rules ignore capital letters when they look for text. Colours appear on this screen only: they are not in Export, CSV, the chart or any other app. Cells also say which colour they have in their tooltip. Rules are kept in this browser with the tab, and are not undoable.

      No colour rules on this tab.

      Cell format

      A format rule makes cells in a range bold, aligned left, centre or right, and shows plain numbers with a number format (whole number, 2 decimals, thousands with 2 decimals, or percent with 1 decimal). It only changes how cells look: the stored value, formulas, sorting, filtering, the chart, the pivot, Export and the CSV all keep the unformatted value. Export (.json) and the CSV do not contain formats, so importing an export brings back the values only and loses the formatting. Text, errors and blank cells are shown as they are. Later rules override earlier ones, one property at a time. A cell's tooltip shows the value behind a number format. Rules are kept in this browser with the tab and are not undoable. Colour rules compare the unformatted value. Once a format rule has been saved, the tab list of this file is marked so that an older copy of this page refuses it (it says the stored tab list could not be read and leaves your data alone). If a window you opened before this update says that, reload it.

        No format rules on this tab.

        Data validation

        A rule limits what can be typed into a range on this tab: a list of allowed values or a number from a smallest to a largest. It is checked when you type a value into a cell and leave it. It does not check formulas, blank cells, values already there, Import, Sort, Undo or Clear, and it is not in the Export or CSV files. Rules are kept in this browser with the tab.
